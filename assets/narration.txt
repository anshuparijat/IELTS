SLIDE 1 — INTRODUCTION
In this lesson, we will analyse an IELTS Academic Writing Task One table. The table compares the percentages of household spending on four categories in Canada, Japan, Mexico and Germany in 2024. Our task is to identify the strongest patterns, select useful figures and organise the report clearly.

SLIDE 2 — READ THE TABLE
Read the categories across the four countries. Housing accounts for 28 percent in Canada, 24 in Japan, 20 in Mexico and 26 in Germany. Food ranges from 16 percent in Canada to 24 percent in Mexico. Transport ranges from 10 percent in Japan to 14 percent in Mexico. Other items account for 44 percent in Canada, 48 in Japan, 42 in Mexico and 45 in Germany. Each country's figures add up to 100 percent.

SLIDE 3 — HIGHEST FIGURES
The clearest pattern is that other items represent the largest share in every country. Japan records the highest proportion, at 48 percent. Germany follows at 45 percent, Canada stands at 44 percent, and Mexico has 42 percent. For housing, Canada records the highest figure, at 28 percent. Notice how we can rank the values and select the most useful comparisons instead of describing every number separately.

SLIDE 4 — LOWEST FIGURES
Transport represents the smallest share in all four countries. The figure is lowest in Japan, at 10 percent. Canada and Germany both record 12 percent, while Mexico has 14 percent. Food is also relatively low compared with other items and housing. Its share ranges from 16 percent in Canada to 24 percent in Mexico. This contrast gives us enough evidence for the second body paragraph.

SLIDE 5 — FULL SAMPLE ANSWER
The introduction paraphrases the question by naming the subject, categories, countries and year. The overview identifies the two dominant features: other items account for the largest share in all four countries, while transport has the smallest proportion throughout. Body paragraph one groups the highest figures, covering other items and then housing. Body paragraph two groups the lowest categories, covering transport and food. A useful expression is “ranging from 16 percent in Canada to 24 percent in Mexico”. Also, use “highest figures” and “lowest figures”, rather than “highest data” or “lowest data”, because figures is the natural countable noun here.

SLIDE 6 — KEY TAKEAWAY
Remember this structure: introduction, overview, body paragraph one and body paragraph two. Use accurate percentage vocabulary such as accounted for, represented, and made up. Select the highest and lowest values, plus other key comparisons. Group related information logically: other items and housing in the first body paragraph, then transport and food in the second. Do not list every number mechanically. Compare the data, highlight the main patterns, and keep the report concise and well organised.
